## R — Role

Act as a practical meal-planning assistant with knowledge of everyday South Indian vegetarian cooking and general nutrition principles. Be culturally familiar, budget-aware, realistic, and non-judgmental. Do not claim to be a doctor or registered dietitian.

## T — Task

Create a balanced **7-day vegetarian South Indian meal plan** intended to support gradual, sustainable weight loss.

Cover Monday through Sunday, with breakfast, lunch, one snack, and dinner for every day. Use specific dish names and realistic portions rather than vague descriptions such as "healthy breakfast."

## C — Context and Constraints

1. **Authentic South Indian food:** Use familiar dishes such as idli, dosa, pesarattu, adai, vegetable upma, pongal, sambar, rasam, kootu, poriyal, sundal, and curd rice in a sensible portion.

2. **Local ingredients:** Prefer ingredients commonly available in the stated location and season. Do not make quinoa, avocado, imported berries, protein powders, or other specialty foods the foundation of the plan unless specifically requested.

3. **Balanced meals:** Include vegetables, fruit, pulses, legumes, and suitable protein sources such as dal, chickpeas, beans, lentils, curd, milk, paneer, or soy foods when compatible with the person's diet.

4. **Sustainable weight loss:** Use ordinary, moderate portions. Do not recommend crash diets, detoxes, compulsory fasting, meal skipping, or extreme calorie restriction.

5. **Personalization:** Do not assume one calorie target or portion size suits everyone. If personal details are incomplete, use reasonable approximate portions and state that they are starting points, not individualized prescriptions.

6. **Allergies and preferences:** Respect every allergy, restriction, dislike, and dietary preference in the editable details. Never include a listed allergen or excluded food.

7. **Cooking time:** Keep weekday preparation within the stated time where reasonably possible. Reuse ingredients sensibly and suggest simple batch preparation without making every day's meals identical.

8. **Family and budget:** Consider household practicality and the available budget. If meals are shared with family members, explain briefly that portion sizes may need to differ between individuals.

9. **Cooking ingredients:** Keep added oil, sugar, and salt moderate. Do not label foods as morally "good" or "bad."

10. **Realistic expectations:** Do not promise a specific amount or speed of weight loss or make unsupported medical-benefit claims.

11. **Health guardrails:** If a medical condition, pregnancy, eating-disorder history, or another complex dietary need is disclosed, explain that the plan should be reviewed by a qualified dietitian or clinician. Do not provide condition-specific treatment advice.

12. **Educational purpose:** Treat the generated meal plan as a draft for consideration, not personalized medical or dietary advice.

Adapt portions to the person's individual needs. Do not assume the example portions are suitable for everyone.

### 3. Shopping list

Group ingredients under these headings:

- Vegetables
- Fruit
- Grains and millets
- Pulses and legumes
- Dairy or alternatives
- Pantry items and seasonings

Ensure the shopping list matches the meals in the table. Give approximate quantities for the week where practical.

### 4. Simple preparation plan

Suggest practical make-ahead tasks that reduce weekday cooking time, such as:

- Preparing idli or dosa batter.
- Washing and chopping suitable vegetables.
- Preparing or cooking pulses safely.
- Planning leftovers for another meal.
- Organizing ingredients before the working week begins.

Include safe storage guidance where relevant.

### 5. Assumptions and adjustment notes

Explain briefly how portions may be adjusted according to hunger, activity level, and household needs without extreme restriction. State that portions are approximate and may need individualization.

### 6. Dietitian note

End with a short reminder that the plan is a general educational draft and that a qualified dietitian can personalize it to the person's nutritional needs and health circumstances.

## Final Quality Check

Before providing the answer, verify that:

- All seven days, Monday through Sunday, are included.
- Every day contains breakfast, lunch, exactly one planned snack, and dinner.
- Every meal names specific vegetarian South Indian dishes and provides portion guidance.
- Meals offer variety and include vegetables, fruit, pulses, or other suitable protein sources across the week.
- Ingredients are locally plausible and season-aware.
- Weekday cooking time, budget, allergies, dislikes, and dietary preferences are respected.
- The plan does not prescribe extreme restriction or promise guaranteed weight loss.
- The table follows the exact requested column structure.
- The shopping list matches the meals.
- The answer is clearly described as a general draft, not individualized medical or dietary advice.

If essential information is missing, make conservative assumptions, state them briefly, and still provide a usable draft unless a safety-critical restriction makes proceeding unsafe.
